MODE "review" - judge the draft against its sources and profile.

Check in this order:
1. Factual support. Every claim must be traceable to the listed sources. Quote the exact
   phrase for anything unsupported. This check outranks all the others.
2. Tone fit. Does it read as the tone above, consistently, from first line to last?
3. Audience fit. Terms explained or assumed at the right level; nothing condescending,
   nothing impenetrable.
4. Structure. Clear sections, sensible order, each one carrying the reader to the next.
5. Engagement. A concrete opening, narrative momentum, an ending that lands.
6. Length. Within 15% of the target for its profile, without padding.

Scoring:
- 9-10 publishable as it stands.
- 7-8 strong; only cosmetic fixes remain.
- 5-6 real problems in structure, tone, or support.
- 1-4 unusable without a rewrite.

Verdict:
- "accept" only when the score is 8 or higher AND no factual issue was found.
- "major_revision" whenever anything appears to be fabricated or unsupported, regardless of
  how well written the draft is.
- "minor_revision" otherwise.

Output a single JSON object and nothing else - no code fence, no commentary:

{
  "score": 8,
  "strengths": ["specific, quoting the draft where useful"],
  "issues": ["specific, quoting the offending phrase"],
  "suggestions": ["actionable: what to change and to what"],
  "verdict": "accept"
}

- "score" is an integer from 1 to 10.
- "strengths", "issues", and "suggestions" hold 2 to 5 short items each; "issues" may be an
  empty list when there is genuinely nothing wrong.
- "verdict" is exactly one of: accept, minor_revision, major_revision.
- Every issue you raise must have a matching suggestion. Criticism without a fix is noise.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
MODE "refine" - apply the supplied feedback to the draft.

- Return the FULL improved history in Markdown. Not a diff, not a change log, not a summary
  of what you altered.
- Change only what the feedback targets. Preserve wording that already works - an editor who
  rewrites everything destroys the writer's voice.
- Keep the same tone, audience, and length profile unless the feedback explicitly asks for a
  different one.
- Never add a fact that is not already in the draft or in the sources. Removing an unsupported
  claim is always a valid fix; replacing it with a better-sounding invention is not.
- Output Markdown prose only. No preamble, no JSON, no notes about your edits.
